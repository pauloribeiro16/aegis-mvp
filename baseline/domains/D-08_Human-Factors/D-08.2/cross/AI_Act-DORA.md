**Scope comparison:**
- DORA scope: compulsory ICT security awareness + digital operational resilience training (workforce, `commensurate to the remit`).
- AI Act scope: deployer assignment of human oversight to competent persons (5-element abilities + competence/training/authority/support).
- Scope overlap: **Y (when the DORA financial entity deploys high-risk AI).**

**Trigger comparison:**
- DORA trigger: compulsory-modules-in-training-schemes.
- AI Act trigger: deployer assignment of human oversight.
- Trigger alignment: **PARTIAL** (both staff-targeted but different content).

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- DORA: `compulsory` + `commensurate to the remit`.
- AI Act: `as appropriate and proportionate` + 5-element cumulative.
- Tension: **NONE**.

**Recipient comparison:**
- DORA: management body, NCA.
- AI Act: deployer, MSA.

**Content template comparison:**
- DORA: function-specific depth.
- AI Act: 5-element + competence/training/authority/support.

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** COMPLEMENTARY
**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** **SAME — COMPLEMENTARY**
**Reasoning:** DORA's `commensurate to the remit` covers all staff functions including AI-related ones; AI Act adds the explicit 5-element + competence/training/authority/support set for high-risk AI. For DORA financial entities deploying high-risk AI, both apply and stack.

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? **Y (when the DORA financial entity deploys high-risk AI)**. No reclassification required.

**Downstream implication (Phase 1C Doc 07):** Phase 2 obligation derivation must produce a DORA-led stack: DORA Art. 13(6) `commensurate to the remit` carrier + AI Act Art. 14(4) + Art. 26(2) AI-overseer competence as an additional sub-component.
