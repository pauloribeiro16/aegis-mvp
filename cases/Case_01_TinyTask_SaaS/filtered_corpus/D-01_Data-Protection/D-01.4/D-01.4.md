##### Pair: GDPR ↔ CRA

**GDPR article (verbatim — SR-GDPR-005, Art. 5(1)(d) + Art. 5(1)(f)):**
> Art. 5(1)(d) personal data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date; every reasonable step must be taken to ensure that personal data that are inaccurate, having regard to the purposes for which they are processed, are erased or rectified without delay.

**CRA article (verbatim — SR-CRA-005, Annex I Part I (2)(f) + (l)):**
> Annex I Part I (2)(f) protect the integrity of stored, transmitted or otherwise processed data — personal or other — as well as the integrity of commands, programs and configuration against any manipulation or modification not authorised by the user. The manufacturer shall also report on corruptions.

**Scope comparison:**
- GDPR: accuracy (semantic) of personal data + integrity of personal data.
- CRA: integrity of data + commands + programs + configuration (technical, universal-quantifier "any manipulation").
- Scope overlap: Y when CRA product handles personal data; the GDPR accuracy leg (semantic) is a controller obligation beyond CRA's technical-integration scope.

**Trigger comparison:**
- GDPR: continuous + on data-subject request (Art. 16 rectification) + on material change.
- CRA: continuous + corruption-event reporting (triggered on corruption).
- Trigger alignment: PARTIAL — CRA's corruption-reporting is event-triggered; GDPR's rectification is event-triggered (Art. 16 request) and continuous (Art. 5(1)(d) accuracy duty).

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- GDPR: Art. 5(1)(d) "without delay" (VAG; reading chosen: harmonised with Art. 16 "without undue delay").
- CRA: "without delay" not explicit on data integrity; "report corruptions" timing depends on the corruption event.
- Tension: NONE on threshold; CRA's "report corruptions" introduces an event-driven cadence that GDPR's accuracy duty does not match.

**Recipient comparison:**
- GDPR: SA + data subject (Art. 16).
- CRA: user / administering party (per SR-CRA-005 R1 reading; not an external authority).

**Content template comparison:**
- GDPR: Art. 16 rectification cascade + Art. 19 cascade to recipients.
- CRA: corruption reporting in Annex VII + Annex I (2)(l) logging.

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** COMPLEMENTARY.

**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** SAME (CORRECTED — wording only).

**Reasoning:** CRA's technical-integrity universal quantifier is the storage/protection leg; GDPR's semantic-accuracy + Art. 16 rectification cascade is the workflow + downstream leg. The two complement each other on the same data asset: CRA's PR.DS-01 + PR.DS-10 + PR.PS-04 satisfies the storage/technical leg; GDPR's Art. 16 + Art. 19 cascade satisfies the workflow/semantic leg. They are layered on the same data asset but at different layers, with PARTIAL trigger overlap (CRA corruption-event vs GDPR rectification-request).

**Scope-disjoint test:** Y on the same product handling personal data.

**Downstream implication:** Stack-merge with two layers. A CRA-compliant integrity/logging stack (cryptographic hashes + write-audit logging + corruption reports) discharges the GDPR Art. 5(1)(f) integrity leg; the controller separately operates the Art. 5(1)(d) accuracy + Art. 16 rectification workflow + Art. 19 cascade.

**Objective.** Data integrity is preserved against unauthorised modification
across the data lifecycle — at rest, in transit, and in use — with
corruption detected, logged, and reported, and with semantic accuracy
maintained as the workflow leg of the personal-data principle.

**Considerations.**
- The four participating regulations converge on the same substantive
  obligation (integrity of data) but each operates at a distinct object
  layer on the same data asset: GDPR's semantic accuracy + storage integrity
  (Art. 5(1)(d) accuracy + Art. 16 rectification cascade + Art. 5(1)(f)
  integrity), CRA's technical integrity with universal-quantifier protection
  (Annex I (2)(f) integrity of data + commands + programs + configuration
  against `any manipulation or modification not authorised by the user` +
  (2)(l) corruption reporting), DORA's ICT-system CIA+A integrity cell
  (Art. 9(2) 4-way CIA+A × 3-way data states), and AI Act's AI-system
  resilience (Art. 15(3) `as resilient as possible regarding errors, faults
  or inconsistencies` with backup/fail-safe plans as one permissible means).

  inside GDPR personal-data accuracy, which is a sub-property of AI Act
  system resilience.

  rectification workflow (Art. 16 cascade + Art. 19 recipients notification)

#### Scope-overlap principle (operational). The high-level SO
(`SO-D-01.4.HL`) engages on a given data asset **if and only if ≥2
applicable regulations' primary-object scopes overlap on the same data
asset** (e.g. personal data stored on a CRA-regulated product deployed by
a DORA financial entity running a high-risk AI workload that processes
the personal data — a vertically-integrated manufacturer-controller on a
financial-services stack with an AI-system layer above). The nested-object
framing means the engagement is **layered**: the high-level SO describes the
common obligation, and the four sub-SOs each anchor a distinct object layer

deploying high-risk AI that processes personal data, the high-level SO
engages on all four layers (personal-data accuracy + product-side

**Why Complementary**: GDPR anchors integrity to two distinct principles (Art. 5(1)(d) accuracy — semantic; Art. 5(1)(f) integrity — storage) and Art. 16 rectification (cascade workflow). CRA anchors to a single clause covering data + commands + programs + configuration with a universal-quantifier "any manipulation" and a corruption-reporting obligation. The two are complementary: GDPR's accuracy dimension is semantic; CRA's integrity dimension is technical. A CRA product handling personal data is jointly bound; the CRA universal-quantifier integrity protection satisfies GDPR's Art. 5(1)(f) integrity leg, while GDPR's Art. 5(1)(d) accuracy leg remains a controller obligation. → full rationale: `../../../Regulation/GDPR/02_SecurityRules_NIST.md` SR-GDPR-005 + SR-GDPR-006; `../../../Regulation/CRA/02_SecurityRules_NIST.md` SR-CRA-005


<!-- /aegis:pair -->






##### Emergent tensions (3+ regulations)
<!-- emergent: GDPR,CRA,DORA,AI_Act -->

- The four regulations have **different primary objects for integrity**: GDPR (personal data semantic accuracy), CRA (data + commands + programs + configuration technical integrity), DORA (ICT system CIA+A integrity), AI Act (AI system resilience to errors/faults/inconsistencies). The objects are nested: CRA integrity sits inside DORA CIA+A, which sits inside GDPR personal-data accuracy, which is a sub-property of AI Act's system resilience. A harmonised compliance system must document each object separately rather than collapsing the four into a single "integrity" claim.
- **NIS 2 absence.** All four other regulations impose an integrity obligation; NIS 2 is silent on the standalone integrity dimension (T3-vs-text gap; CRA + DORA are dedicated authorities). For a NIS 2 NSIE that is not also DORA-scoped, the integrity obligation is anchored to NIS 2's broader risk-management framework rather than to a dedicated integrity clause. This is a **regulatory gap** that the AEGIS taxonomy should surface for Layer 2 review.

<!-- /emergent -->

##### SR cross-validation
CSF mappings converge on PR.DS-01 (data-at-rest) and PR.DS-10 (data-in-use) for the data integrity property. CRA additionally anchors on PR.PS-04 (logging); DORA on PR.DS-12 (risk-strategy); AI Act on PR.IR-03 + PR.IR-04 (resilience) + PR.DS-11 (data backup consistency). GDPR adds PR.DS-12 (data managed consistent with risk strategy) for the accuracy-side workflow. The shared PR.DS-01 + PR.DS-10 anchor is the operational pivot; divergence is at the supplementary layer (semantic accuracy / technical integrity / system resilience).

##### Downstream implication
For Phase 1C Doc 07, D-01.4 is a **layered object** sub-domain: the company profile must document integrity at four distinct object levels (personal-data, ICT-system, AI-system, configuration) and the cascade between them. Proportionality: a non-AI, non-DORA SaaS handles the GDPR + CRA floor; a DORA financial entity adds the system-level layer; a DORA financial entity using high-risk AI adds the AI-system layer.

- GDPR: SA + data subject (Art. 16).
- CRA: user / administering party (per SR-CRA-005 R1 reading; not an external authority).

##### Art. 5(1)(c) — Data minimisation

**Clause: GDPR-CL03** | type: principle | obligatedParty: CONTROLLER | obligationType: CONTINUOUS

**Berry anchor:** §3.3.5 (vagueness), §5.1 (`adequate`, `relevant`, `necessary`).

**Instance 1 — VAG / S3 / `adequate`, `relevant`, `necessary`**

Three inquiry-resistant adjectives; no measurement criterion. Art. 5(2) accountability shifts burden of demonstration to controller; Art. 35(7)(b) DPIA requires necessity assessment.

**Variant readings:**
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Adequate iff it serves the designed purpose; necessity presumed by default. | None in OJ; industry self-regulation. |
| R2 | Adequate iff proportionate to purpose; demonstrable ex ante. | Art. 5(2) accountability; Art. 35(7)(b) DPIA. |
| R3 | Adequate iff least-intrusive among equivalent means under Art. 52(1) Charter necessity test. | CJEU C-62/14 Gasket; Charter Art. 52(1). |

R1 vs R3 yield materially distinct compliance obligations (R1 permits default collection patterns; R3 requires per-field justification).

**Instance 2 — SCOPE-Q / S2 / `purposes` (plural)**

`purposes` is plural — the minimisation test applies to each purpose. Berry §5.2.1 (plural-scope `purposes`). S2 because Art. 5(2) accountability and Art. 35(7)(b) DPIA partially anchor the reading.

### Article 21 — Right to object

#### Verbatim (with highlighting)

> **Article 21**
> **Right to object**
>
> 1. The data subject shall have the right to object, on grounds relating to his or her particular situation, at any time to processing of personal data concerning him or her which is based on point (e) or (f) of Article 6(1), including profiling based on those provisions. The controller shall no longer process the personal data unless the controller demonstrates **compelling legitimate grounds** for the processing which override the interests, rights and freedoms of the data subject or for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims.

> 2. Where personal data are processed for direct marketing purposes, the data subject shall have the right to object at any time to processing of personal data concerning him or her for such marketing, which includes profiling to the extent that it is related to such direct marketing.

> 3. Where the data subject objects to processing for direct marketing purposes, the personal data shall no longer be processed for such purposes.

> 4. At the latest at the time of the first communication with the data subject, the right referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 shall be **explicitly brought to the attention of the data subject** and shall be presented clearly and separately from any other information.

> 5. In the context of the use of information society services, and notwithstanding Directive 2002/58/EC, the data subject may exercise his or her right to object by automated means using technical specifications.

> 6. Where personal data are processed for scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes pursuant to Article 89(1), the data subject, on grounds relating to his or her particular situation, shall have the right to object to processing of personal data concerning him or her, unless the processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out for reasons of public interest.
