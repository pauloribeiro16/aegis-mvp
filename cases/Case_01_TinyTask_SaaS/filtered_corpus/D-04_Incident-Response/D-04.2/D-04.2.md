**Objective.** Security incidents affecting the entity's systems, products, services, components and data are contained and mitigated through appropriate measures that address the incident's impact and reduce the probability of further damage.

**Considerations.**
- All four participating regulations (GDPR, NIS 2, CRA, DORA) impose a containment-and-mitigation duty but each targets a distinct containment layer and anchors to a distinct threshold: GDPR's post-breach TOMs (technical and organisational measures) including unintelligibility rendering (Art. 33(3)(d) + Art. 34(3)(a)/(b) unintelligibility carve-out; the unintelligibility rendering is GDPR-unique and acts as a substitute for Art. 34 data-subject notification when applied); NIS 2's entity-side incident-handling `contain` phase within the 6-action lifecycle (Art. 21(2)(b) + Art. 6(8) six-action definition with `contain` as one of the six actions); CRA's product-side availability preservation + cascading-impact minimisation + exploitation mitigation (Annex I Part I (2)(h) DoS resilience + (2)(i) cross-product impact minimisation + (2)(k) exploitation mitigation; the three-clause constellation is CRA-unique and operates at the product-design level); DORA's financial-entity-side BC arrangements with response and recovery plans + backup policies + restoration procedures (Art. 11(1)/(2)/(3) + Art. 12(1); the BC-arrangements architecture is DORA-unique and the strictest containment-architecture floor in the AEGIS corpus).
- The high-level SO surfaces the common containment-and-mitigation duty (contain the incident's impact, mitigate further damage, take measures to address the breach / incident, route to the downstream recovery / notification pipeline); the per-regulation perspective (containment layer, measure type, threshold qualifier, evidence template) is preserved in the four sub-SOs.
- A single organisation across all four regulators must operate a multi-layer containment architecture with explicit layer hand-offs: personal-data runbook feeds entity-level response plan at the controller/NSIE boundary; entity-level response plan feeds product resilience architecture via deployment (CRA products instrument their resilience & exploitation mitigation to support the deployer's containment); product resilience feeds back into DORA BC arrangements via integration. For parties that touch only one regulatory scope, only the triggered sub-SO applies.
- The high-level SO does NOT collapse the four containment layers into a single measure type — the layers contain different objects (personal-data breach impact vs all-hazards entity event impact vs product availability vs ICT infrastructure) and produce different measure types (TOMs incl. unintelligibility render vs lifecycle-phase actions vs product-design choices vs BC arrangements).
- The high-level SO is honesty-preserving about scope-disjoint cases: for a 5-FTE SaaS startup with GDPR + CRA applicability but no NIS 2 / DORA applicability, only the GDPR and CRA sub-SOs activate; the high-level SO engages on the same party if both apply, with the boundary between personal-data runbook (GDPR) and product-resilience architecture (CRA) documented as the controlled hand-off.

**Objective.** Personal-data breaches are contained and mitigated through appropriate technical and organisational measures, including — where applicable — measures rendering the personal data unintelligible to any person who is not authorised to access the data (Art. 34(3)(a)) and subsequent measures that ensure the high risk no longer materialises (Art. 34(3)(b)); the controller describes the measures taken or proposed in the Art. 33(3)(d) supervisory-authority notification.

**Considerations.**
- The GDPR containment-and-mitigation obligation is the personal-data post-breach runbook; the operational substrate is a TOMs catalogue that the controller applies when a personal-data breach is detected, with the unintelligibility rendering as the GDPR-unique carve-out that operates as a substitute for Art. 34 data-subject notification when applied. The `where appropriate` qualifier from Art. 33(3)(d) propagates and operationalises the TOMs catalogue depth: high-risk data categories (special categories, financial data, health data) trigger deeper and more aggressive containment than low-risk categories.
- The personal-data post-breach measure object is distinct from the NIS 2 entity-level lifecycle-phase actions, the CRA product-design availability choices, and the DORA BC arrangements — the controller-facing post-breach runbook is the containment layer, and the TOMs discipline operates at that layer only.
- For a multi-reg party, the GDPR TOMs are documented as a section within the NIS 2 entity-level response plan (Doc 07 downstream implication) or within the DORA BC arrangements (Doc 07 downstream implication), not as a separate runbook.
- The downstream notification cascade (Art. 33 supervisory-authority notification including the measures description) is part of D-04.3, not D-04.2.

PR.DS-12, PR.IR-04, RC.RP-04.

**Objective.** The availability of essential and basic functions of a product with digital elements is protected also after an incident, through resilience and mitigation measures including resistance to and recovery from denial-of-service attacks (Annex I Part I (2)(h)); the manufacturer minimises the negative impact by the product itself or connected devices on the availability of services provided by other devices or networks (Annex I Part I (2)(i)); the manufacturer reduces the impact of an incident using appropriate exploitation-mitigation mechanisms and techniques (Annex I Part I (2)(k)). Where the manufacturer knows or has reason to believe that the product or processes are not in conformity with the Annex I cybersecurity requirements, the manufacturer takes the corrective measures necessary to bring the product into conformity, or to withdraw or recall the product (Art. 13(21)).

**Considerations.**
- The CRA containment-and-mitigation obligation is the product-level post-incident availability + cross-product impact + exploitation mitigation architecture; the operational substrate is a product-design framework that combines DoS resilience (Annex I Part I (2)(h)), cascading-impact minimisation (Annex I Part I (2)(i)), and exploitation-mitigation mechanisms (Annex I Part I (2)(k)). The three-clause constellation is CRA-unique and operates at the product-design level — the containment happens inside the product boundary.
- The product-design measure object is distinct from the GDPR personal-data post-breach runbook (post-event reaction), the NIS 2 entity-level lifecycle-phase actions (entity boundary), and the DORA BC arrangements (financial-entity infrastructure) — the product boundary is internal to the product, and the design-level resilience discipline operates at that boundary only.
- The CRA product's Annex I (2)(h)/(i)/(k) architecture supports entity-level NIS 2 containment by reducing blast radius; for a DORA financial entity deploying CRA products, the CRA resilience design feeds the DORA BC arrangements. The CRA containment is pre-event design + post-incident availability preservation, temporally distinct from GDPR's post-event reaction; the high-level SO does not collapse the temporal distinction.
- The downstream notification cascade (Annex I Part II (4)/(5) CSIRT + ENISA CVD notification; Art. 14(1)/(3) AEV/SI notification including corrective measures) is part of D-04.3, not D-04.2.

### 4. Cross-clause structural observations (Art. 13)

1. **Art. 13(2) is the operational core of the manufacturer's risk-assessment duty.** The 6-phase lifecycle propagation (CL16) is the **longest single coordination pattern in CRA** — 6 phases coordinated by AND. Berry §5.5 parallelism admits partial-compliance readings.

3. **Art. 13(5) is the supply-chain due-diligence hook (Q6=b split).** T2 misattributed this clause to Art. 13(13)'s 10-year documentation retention. The v0.2 split places the actual Art. 13(5) due-diligence analysis here (CL23a) and the 10-year retention in CL38.

4. **Art. 13(2) sentence 2 is CRA-unique.** The `health and safety of users` clause bridges cybersecurity to physical safety. No other regulation in the corpus (GDPR, NIS 2, DORA, AI Act) has this.

5. **`reasonable user expectations` (CL28) is the cross-regulation Berry-classic.** Same term-pattern as `reasonable foreseeability` in Art. 3(24)/(25). Layer 1 catalogues; Layer 2 must adjudicate against product-liability case law.

6. **6 S3 clauses (CL15, CL16, CL18, CL19, CL23a, CL27, CL28, CL33, CL42):** risk assessment, 6-phase propagation, documentation, intended purpose + foreseeability, due diligence, support-period factors, 10-year updates, single point of contact. **No S1 instances.**
