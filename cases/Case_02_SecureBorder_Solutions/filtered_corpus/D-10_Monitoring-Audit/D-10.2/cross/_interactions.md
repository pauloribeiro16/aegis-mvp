## Cross-recipient map

#### Cross-recipient map
D-10.2 generates a layered recipient set on the **audit-records evidence trail**. The recipients are at distinct regulatory channels on distinct record types but the channels do not generate a notification cascade of D-10.2 itself — they are **audit-records channels** that feed distinct regulatory artefacts on the same record object (or different record objects in scope-disjoint pairs):
- **GDPR** — data subject + supervisory authority (DPA) on the Art. 30(3) compliance records + Art. 5(2) accountability evidence + Art. 31 SA cooperation (records `made available to the supervisory authority on request`).
- **CRA** — market surveillance authority (MSA) on the Annex VII §5–§8 technical documentation + Annex VII §3 risk-assessment documentation + Art. 13(4) sentence 1 + Art. 13(22) MSA cooperation (`on a reasoned request from a market surveillance authority, where necessary to check compliance`).
- **DORA** — national competent authority (NCA) on the Art. 9(4)(a) information security policy (CIA+A rules + logging implicit).
- **AI Act** — provider + market surveillance authority (MSA) on the Art. 12(1)+(2) automatic event logging + Art. 19(1) provider log retention (provider keeps logs under their control; MSA cooperation per Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 via Art. 74).
These are distinct regulatory channels on the audit-records object. **The cross-recipient map does NOT generate a notification cascade of D-10.2 itself** — the audit-logging duty is upstream of any breach notification (cross to D-04.3 Regulatory Notification) and the D-09.4 Records of Processing documentation obligation. **The N (typically) GDPR ↔ CRA pair has no recipient overlap** — the GDPR compliance records go to DPA, the CRA product documentation goes to MSA on different record types (substantively scope-disjoint). **The floor-within-ceiling pattern (GDPR ↔ AI_Act) has no recipient overlap** — the AI Act provider log retention feeds the MSA; the GDPR compliance records feed the DPA. The two record objects are different but reconciled on the time envelope.

## Scope-overlap principle (operational)

#### Scope-overlap principle (operational)
The high-level SO (`SG-D-10.2.HL`) engages on a given party **if and only if ≥2 applicable regulations have ≥1 Y or Conditional-Y-scope-overlap pair on the same party** (e.g. a DORA financial entity that is also a controller with CRA-conformant products deploying high-risk AI — a vertically-integrated financial-controller-manufacturer-AI-provider with GDPR + CRA + DORA + AI Act applicability on the layered 4-audit-records architecture, with the GDPR ↔ CRA pair substantively scope-disjoint by record-type distinctness but the 4-reg stack engages on the joint-condition operator via the Conditional-Y pairs involving DORA and/or AI Act). The four audit-logging mandates are stacked, not collapsed: compliance records (GDPR) + product documentation (CRA) + CIA+A information security policy (DORA) + AI-system event logs (AI Act). The high-level SO surfaces the common audit-logging duty but does **NOT** collapse the per-reg anchor, scope dimension, trigger, threshold, recipient, or content template, **and does NOT need to resolve any emergent tension** because no OJ conflict exists at D-10.2. **NIS 2 does not emit a sub-SO** — fully out-of-scope per CRDA T3-vs-text gap.
