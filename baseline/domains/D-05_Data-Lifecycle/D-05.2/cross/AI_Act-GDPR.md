# D-05.2 — Pair analysis: GDPR ↔ AI Act

> Cross-regulation layer: consult only when BOTH regulations are applicable.
> Canonical source: merged Part 1 §1 (deep) + Part 2 (summary).

##### Pair: GDPR ↔ AI_Act

**GDPR article (verbatim, Art. 5(1)(e)):**
> "Personal data shall be kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data are processed ('storage limitation'). Personal data may be stored for longer periods insofar as the personal data will be processed solely for archiving purposes in the public interest, scientific or historical research purposes or statistical purposes in accordance with Article 89(1) subject to implementation of the appropriate technical and organisational measures required by this Regulation in order to safeguard the rights and freedoms of the data subject." — Art. 5(1)(e) GDPR

**AI_Act article (verbatim, Art. 19(1)):**
> "Providers of high-risk AI systems shall retain the logs referred to in Article 12(1), automatically generated by the system, for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, and of at least six months, unless Union or national law provides otherwise." — AI Act Art. 19(1)

(Note: per AI_Act validation report v0.1 §3.4, SR-AIACT-008 maps to Art. 19(1) with a T5 mapping-error note. The substance of the 6-month log retention is in Art. 19(1); the T5 tag cites Art. 12(4) which does not exist in the OJ. This analysis uses the OJ-literal locus at Art. 19(1).)

**Scope comparison:**
- GDPR scope: personal data held by the controller.
- AI Act scope: automatically-generated logs of high-risk AI systems.
- Scope overlap: **Y (when AI-system logs contain personal data)**.

**Trigger comparison:**
- GDPR trigger: continued storage of personal data beyond processing-purpose end.
- AI Act trigger: high-risk AI system log generation.
- Trigger alignment: **PARTIAL** — AI Act applies for the log lifetime; GDPR applies to any personal data in the log.

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- GDPR: "no longer than is necessary" — qualitative upper bound.
- AI Act: ≥6-month minimum floor.
- Tension: **NONE — they are reconcilable.** The AI Act 6-month floor is a subset of GDPR's "as long as necessary" envelope. If the intended purpose requires 6+ months of logs, GDPR's "necessary" is satisfied for that period. The CRDA reasoning itself states: "The two are reconcilable: the AI Act 6-month floor is a subset of GDPR's 'as long as necessary'."

**Recipient comparison:**
- GDPR: data subject + DPA.
- AI Act: market surveillance authority (MSA) + national competent authority.

**Content template comparison:**
- GDPR: not codified; documented in RoPA (Art. 30(1)(f)).
- AI Act: not codified for content; Art. 19(1) sets the floor.

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** CONTRADICTORY (direction)
**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** **CORRECTED — COMPLEMENTARY** (floor-within-ceiling)
**Reasoning:** The CRDA reasoning explicitly states: "The two are reconcilable: the AI Act 6-month floor is a subset of GDPR's 'as long as necessary'." A floor-within-ceiling relationship is the textbook **complementary** pattern — one regulation sets a hard floor (≥6 months), the other sets a qualitative ceiling (no longer than necessary), and the operative range is the intersection [6 months, "necessary"]. There is no contradiction. The CRDA "contradictory" label is scope-incorrect. Two regulators that set the same boundary at different sides of an interval do not contradict each other; they jointly bound the admissible range.

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? **Y (when AI-system logs contain personal data, both GDPR and AI Act apply on the same log set at the same controller / provider)**. The two obligations are reconcilable within the intersection [6 months, "necessary"]. The pair is substantive-complementary, not scope-disjoint.

**Downstream implication (Phase 1C Doc 07):** Phase 2 obligation derivation must codify AI Act Art. 19(1) as a *floor* within the GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) *ceiling* envelope. The CRDA "contradictory" label should be corrected to "complementary". Phase 3 case generation must validate that high-risk AI providers set the AI Act 6-month floor as a hard minimum and the GDPR "necessary" boundary as the cap.
