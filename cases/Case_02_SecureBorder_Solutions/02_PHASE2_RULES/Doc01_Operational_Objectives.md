# Privacy & Security Operational Objectives — Case 02 (SecureBorder Solutions)

> **Document Purpose:** Defines the Operational Objectives for SecureBorder Solutions across all 35 active sub-domains, organised domain by domain. Each sub-domain establishes the baseline High-Level Goal (`SG-D-XX.Y.HL`), its high-level Operational Translation for SecureBorder, and a structured tabular specification separating the distinct operational scopes of GDPR (Personal Data protection), CRA (Product & Software Security), NIS 2 (Network & Information Systems security of the essential entity), and the AI Act (High-Risk AI System governance).

---

## §1 Governance & Structure Guide

### 1.1 Separation of Regulatory Ambits (GDPR / CRA / NIS 2 / AI Act)
Where multiple regulations apply to the same sub-domain, they bind **distinct operational ambits** and cannot be conflated into a single objective:
- **GDPR Operational Objectives (`PO-` with GDPR scope):** Focus on the confidentiality, integrity, availability, and rights of **natural persons and personal data** — traveller biometric templates processed on behalf of government authorities (processor role) and employee data (controller role) — under GDPR Art. 5, 9, 25, 32, and Chapter III.
- **CRA Operational Objectives (`SO-` with CRA scope):** Focus on the security, tamper-resistance, and cyber-resilience of the **product with digital elements, its interfaces, configurations, and supply-chain components** against unauthorized exploitation, under CRA Annex I and Art. 13 (Critical Class, notified-body assessment).
- **NIS 2 Operational Objectives (`SO-` with NIS 2 scope):** Focus on the risk-management measures of the **essential entity's network and information systems** — acquisition, development, maintenance, incident handling, supply chain, cryptography, HR security, and training — under NIS 2 Art. 20-23.
- **AI Act Operational Objectives (`SO-` with AI Act scope):** Focus on the **high-risk AI system** lifecycle — risk management, data governance, accuracy and robustness, human oversight, logging, transparency, quality management, and post-market monitoring — under AI Act Art. 9-15, 17, 26, and 72-73.

### 1.2 High-Level Goal Operational Translation
In accordance with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clause 6.2 and ISO 29148, each sub-domain begins by restating the common Baseline Goal (`SG-D-XX.Y.HL`) and providing its **Operational Translation** — the high-level system principle that explains *how* the organization satisfies the goal before descending into testable criteria.

### 1.3 Empirical Verification Methods
Every operational objective is evaluated via one of four empirical methods (INCOSE / ISO 29148):
- `TEST`: Automated assertion, continuous integration test, or runtime security probe.
- `INSPECT`: Systematic review of configuration, process artefacts, or audit records.
- `DEMONSTRATE`: Walkthrough or observable execution of an end-to-end operational flow.
- `ANALYZE`: Systematic risk examination, architectural boundary review, or gap analysis.

## §3 Domain D-01 — Data Protection & Encryption
> **Domain Lead:** `CISO` | **Supporting Roles:** `DPO + Lead Developer` | **Active Sub-Domains:** 4

### D-01.1 — Data at Rest Encryption

**High-Level Baseline Goal (SG-D-01.1.HL):** Data held at rest in storage is protected against unauthorised access through appropriate technical measures — primarily encryption with appropriate key custody — calibrated to the risks identified in the entity's risk assessment and commensurate with the state of the art.

**Operational Translation (SecureBorder Context):** For SecureBorder Solutions (450-person supplier of AI-enabled border-control gates with an EU-region cloud back-office), operationalizing `D-01.1` requires establishing verifiable operational controls that enforce data at rest encryption across the eGate fleet, the on-board verification AI, and the supporting network and information systems, leveraging the certified management system and the 24/7 SOC while maintaining clear separation between the four regulatory ambits (personal data, product security, entity security, AI governance).
