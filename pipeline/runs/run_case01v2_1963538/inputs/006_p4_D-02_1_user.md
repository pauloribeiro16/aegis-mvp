## Cross-recipient map

#### Cross-recipient map
D-02.1 itself does **not** generate recipient asymmetry — the
identification process is inward-looking, with the output (a vulnerability
register / risk catalogue / SBOM) feeding downstream disclosure channels.
The recipient fragmentation occurs at the **incident-reporting sub-domain
D-04.3**: the same vulnerability that D-02.1 identifies can trigger five
distinct notification cascades (GDPR Art. 33 to SA, NIS 2 Art. 23(4) to
CSIRT, CRA Art. 14(1)/(3) to CSIRT + ENISA, DORA Art. 19 to CA, AI Act Art.
73 to MSA). For a single dual-scope event, the identification substrate is
unified; the notification cascade is layered. The D-04.3 sub-domain is the
canonical recipient-fragmentation locus for the vulnerability-management
area.

## HSO considerations (from D-02.1.0 — High-level SecurityObjective)

**Considerations.**
- The five participating regulations impose the same substantive
  identification duty at different scope-determiners (personal data /
  network and information systems / product with digital elements /
  ICT-supported business functions / high-risk AI system), different
  temporal floors (GDPR `regularly` (Art. 32(1)(d) + Art. 35(11), VAG) /
  NIS 2 silent baseline (Art. 21(2)(e), no temporal anchor on
  identification per `99_NIS2_Synthesis.md` §8) / CRA R2 `constructive
  knowledge` baseline (Annex I Part I (2)(a) + Part II (1), inquiry-resistant
  `what the manufacturer exercising Annex VII §3 due diligence should
  know`) / DORA `continuous + at-least-yearly` hard anchor (Art. 7(2)
  sentence 2 — the strictest temporal floor of the five) / AI Act
  `known AND reasonably foreseeable` risk-identification cycle (Art.
  9(2)(a)–(d) + Art. 15(4)–(5))), and — for the AI Act — a distinct
  threat-model layer (AI-specific attack categories alongside
  conventional cybersecurity) with no analog in the other four
  regulations' conventional vulnerability models.
- CRDA-deep verified all 10 pairs as SAME: a single identification
  programme calibrated to the strictest temporal floor (DORA's
  `continuous + at-least-yearly`) discharges GDPR's `regularly` + NIS 2's
  silent baseline + CRA's R2 `constructive knowledge` on the same
  asset; the AI Act AI-specific attack taxonomy is a layered overlay
  added when the asset is a high-risk AI system — not contradictory
  but stacked on the conventional identification programme.
- The per-regulation perspective (scope-determiner, temporal anchor,
  threat model, recipient, content template) is preserved in the five
  sub-SOs; the high-level SO surfaces the common obligation only.
- For assets that touch only one regulatory scope, only the triggered
  sub-SO applies; on pure-financial entities where DORA is lex specialis,
  DORA absorbs the NIS 2 identification obligation and the high-level SO
  does not aggregate across the lex-specialis boundary.

#### Scope-overlap principle (operational). The high-level SO
(`SG-D-02.1.HL`) engages on a given asset **if and only if ≥2 applicable
regulations' scope-determiners overlap on the same asset** (e.g. personal
data at a NIS 2 essential entity that is also a CRA-regulated product on
a DORA financial entity running a high-risk AI workload — a
vertically-integrated manufacturer-controller on a financial-services stack
with an AI-system layer above). Pure co-applicability of disjoint-scope
regulations on different assets does **not** trigger cross-regulation
aggregation — see the Activation logic in the file header. For a 5-FTE SaaS
startup with GDPR + CRA applicability but no NIS 2 / DORA / AI Act
applicability, only the GDPR and CRA sub-SOs activate; the high-level SO
engages on the same asset if both apply. **The AI Act AI-specific threat
model (Art. 15(4)–(5)) is a layered overlay, not a tension**: it sits on
top of the conventional cybersecurity identification programme; for an
entity in scope of the AI Act, both legs run — the conventional ID.RA-01
register + the AI-specific attack-taxonomy overlay — without contradiction.
