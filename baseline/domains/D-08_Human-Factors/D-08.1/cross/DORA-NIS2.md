**Scope comparison:**
- NIS 2 scope: entity-wide basic cyber-hygiene baseline (workforce).
- DORA scope: compulsory ICT security awareness + digital operational resilience training (workforce).
- Scope overlap: **Y (when a DORA financial entity is also an NSIE).**

**Trigger comparison:**
- NIS 2 trigger: entity-side hygiene training.
- DORA trigger: compulsory-modules-in-training-schemes requirement.
- Trigger alignment: **SAME** (both workforce awareness at entity level).

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- NIS 2: no quantitative cadence.
- DORA: `compulsory` (completion-mandatory).
- Tension: **NONE**.

**Recipient comparison:**
- NIS 2: management body, competent authority.
- DORA: management body, NCA.

**Content template comparison:**
- NIS 2: not codified.
- DORA: `commensurate to the remit of their functions`.

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** EQUAL
**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** **SAME — EQUAL**
**Reasoning:** Both regulations impose workforce awareness programmes at the entity level. DORA's `compulsory modules` is more prescriptive than NIS 2's `basic cyber hygiene` (which is entity-discretionary under Recital 56). For financial entities, DORA is the dominant authority; for non-financial NSIEs, NIS 2 is the authority.

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? **Y (when the DORA financial entity is also an NSIE applying workforce awareness)**. No reclassification required.

**Downstream implication (Phase 1C Doc 07):** Phase 2 obligation derivation must produce a DORA-dominant stack: DORA Art. 13(6) compulsory-modules discharges both DORA's dedicated awareness obligation and NIS 2's Art. 21(2)(g) baseline for DORA financial entities.
