**Objective.** Providers of high-risk AI systems keep the logs referred to in Art. 12(1) — automatically generated by their high-risk AI systems and to the extent such logs are under their control — for a period appropriate to the intended purpose, of at least six months, unless provided otherwise in the applicable Union or national law, in particular Union law on the protection of personal data (Art. 19(1)).

- The `under their control` qualifier is the responsibility-allocation hinge (physical control + legal control via contractual retention obligation + technical access via read-access; cumulative reading).

### from regs/AI_Act.md body

**Considerations.**

- The AI Act ≥6-month floor is a subset of GDPR's "no longer than is necessary" envelope when the log contains personal data — the operative retention range is [6 months, "necessary"] with AI Act setting the floor and GDPR setting the cap (floor-within-ceiling).

### from regs/AI_Act.md body

- The CRA-side coverage of CRA update artefacts and AI Act AI logs is disjoint in baseline — different objects (update artefacts vs AI logs), different actors (manufacturer vs provider), different purposes (user protection continuity vs post-market monitoring).

### from regs/AI_Act.md body

*(PR.PS-04 anchors the log-generation substrate that the 6-month
retention floor requires — without continuous log generation, the
retention duty is vacuous and the post-market monitoring and
incident-investigation flows have no reconstructable trail. The
log-generation duty is in Art. 12(1) (`over the lifetime` VAG,
aligned with the product-lifecycle definition in Art. 3(1)(c)) and
the `events (logs)` POLY (AI-specific events per Art. 12(2)
`functioning of a high-risk AI system` opening) is read broadly to
include inference decisions, model decisions, override events beyond
conventional security logs. GV.PO-02 captures the policy substrate
that the upward adjustment clause (`appropriate to the intended
purpose`) requires — retention duration beyond the 6-month floor is
justified by the system's risk class and documented in the
provider's cybersecurity policies and procedures. The two
subcategories together cover the retention floor (PR.PS-04) and the
retention-justification methodology (GV.PO-02) that Art. 19(1)
requires. The provider documenting PR.PS-04 log-generation
continuity and GV.PO-02 retention-procedure documentation can
demonstrate ex post that the Art. 19(1) 6-month floor is
operationalised, with documented justification for any upward
adjustment under the `appropriate to the intended purpose` clause
and the cross-regulation interplay with GDPR Art. 5(1)(e), NIS 2
incident-log retention and DORA Art. 12.)*
