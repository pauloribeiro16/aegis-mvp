##### Pair: GDPR ↔ CRA

**GDPR article (verbatim — SR-GDPR-003 + SR-GDPR-004, Art. 4(5) + Art. 32(1)(a) + Art. 34(3)(a)):**
> Art. 4(5) pseudonymisation … the personal data can no longer be attributed to a specific data subject without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately and is subject to appropriate technical and organisational measures. Art. 34(3)(a) … render the personal data unintelligible to any person who is not authorised to access it, such as encryption.

**CRA article (verbatim — SR-CRA-004, Annex I Part I (2)(d) + (2)(e)):**
> Annex I Part I (2)(e) … by encrypting … and by using other technical means. Annex I Part I (2)(d) protect from unauthorised access by appropriate control mechanisms.

**Scope comparison:**
- GDPR: personal-data pseudonymisation/encryption with separate-storage test (Art. 4(5)); unintelligibility carve-out (Art. 34(3)(a)).
- CRA: cryptographic key separation from operational environment (custodian ≠ consumer), anchored via the (2)(d) RBAC + (2)(e) "other technical means" reading.
- Scope overlap: Y when the CRA product protects personal data.

**Trigger comparison:**
- GDPR: continuous + breach-context (Art. 34(3)(a) carve-out triggered by breach).
- CRA: design-time invariant (separation is a property of the product).
- Trigger alignment: PARTIAL — GDPR's Art. 34(3)(a) is breach-triggered; CRA's separation is continuous. The trigger alignment is partial because one obligation is breach-triggered (the carve-out) and the other is design-time.

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- GDPR: "appropriate technical and organisational measures" + unintelligibility "render … unintelligible to any person who is not authorised" (Art. 34(3)(a) "such as encryption").
- CRA: functional-RBAC separation (R2 reading per SR-CRA-004 ambiguity_notes).
- Tension: NONE — the two converge on key separation as the technical property and "unauthorised party cannot access" as the operational test.

**Recipient comparison:**
- GDPR: SA on inspection.
- CRA: MSA.

**Content template comparison:**
- GDPR: Art. 5(2) accountability burden + Art. 32(1)(a) documented measure.
- CRA: Annex VII §2/§3 technical documentation + Annex VII §6 test evidence.

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** COMPLEMENTARY.

**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** SAME (CORRECTED — wording only).

**Reasoning:** Both impose key separation (GDPR Art. 4(5) de-attribution test + Art. 34(3)(a) unintelligibility carve-out ↔ CRA Annex I (2)(d)+(e) functional-RBAC separation) as the technical property; they differ on the trigger (breach-context carve-out vs design-time invariant) and on the documentation artefact (Art. 32 documentation vs Annex VII conformity dossier). On a single product that handles personal data, both are met by the same key-separation architecture; the documentation is the only difference.

**Scope-disjoint test:** Y on the same product; disjoint in baseline.

**Downstream implication:** Stack-merge with documentation. A single key-custody architecture (HSM-anchored, RBAC-separated) plus the dual documentation pack (Art. 32 + Annex VII) discharges both.

**Objective.** Cryptographic keys and related authentication material are
managed with separation of roles such that possession of the key material
is not bundled with possession of the operational environment that uses it;
the key custodian and the data consumer are distinct roles under distinct
technical and organisational control, with the cryptographic key (not the
storage medium) as the necessary condition for intelligibility of the
protected data.

**Considerations.**
- The three participating regulations converge on the same substantive
  obligation (key separation as the technical property) but each
  operationalises it at a distinct depth on the same data asset: GDPR's
  de-attribution test (Art. 4(5) "additional information kept separately and
  subject to appropriate technical and organisational measures"; Art.
  34(3)(a) "render unintelligible to any person who is not authorised to
  access it"), CRA's functional-RBAC separation (Annex I (2)(d)
  access-control + (2)(e) "other technical means" reading per `SR-CRA-004`
  ambiguity_notes), and DORA's classification-driven entity-level
  architecture (Art. 9(4)(d) 3-control AND of strong authentication + key
  protection + encryption, classification-driven cipher strength with
  HSM-anchored custodianship + split-knowledge ceremonies + rotation
  cadence scaled to data criticality).
- CRDA-deep verified all 3 pairs as SAME — a single key-custody
  architecture (HSM-anchored custodianship + functional RBAC + documented
  rotation) discharges all three obligations on the same key material: the
  GDPR Art. 4(5) de-attribution test is met by the separation, the CRA
  Annex I (2)(d)+(e) functional-RBAC separation is met by the role-distinct
  credential assignment, and the DORA Art. 9(4)(d) classification-driven
  tiering is met by the tiered rotation cadence.
- The high-level SO describes the common obligation (key separation); the
  per-regulation perspective is preserved in the three sub-SOs (depth
  anchor, scope qualifier, recipient for compliance demonstration).
- For artefacts touching only one regulatory scope, only the triggered
  sub-SO applies.

#### Scope-overlap principle (operational). The high-level SO
(`SO-D-01.3.HL`) engages on a given key-custody architecture **if and only if
≥2 applicable regulations' scopes overlap on the same key material** (e.g.
the key material protecting personal data on a CRA-regulated product
deployed by a DORA financial entity — a vertically-integrated
manufacturer-controller on a financial-services stack). Pure co-applicability
of disjoint-scope regulations on different key material does **not** trigger
cross-regulation aggregation — see the Activation logic in the file header.
For a 5-FTE SaaS startup with GDPR + CRA applicability but no DORA
applicability, only the GDPR and CRA sub-SOs activate; the high-level SO
engages on the same key material when both apply. For a DORA financial
entity that is also a GDPR controller, the high-level SO engages on the
classification-driven tiering + de-attribution test pair on the same key
material; the DORA depth layer (HSM + split-knowledge + quorum-recovery)
sits above the GDPR/CRA floor.

**Objective.** Cryptographic keys and related authentication material used
to protect the confidentiality and integrity of stored, transmitted or
otherwise processed data by a product with digital elements — personal or
other — are managed with separation of roles such that possession of the
key material is not bundled with possession of the operational environment
that uses it (Annex I Part I (2)(d) access-control + (2)(e) "other technical
means" reading per `SR-CRA-004` ambiguity_notes; the Annex I text does not
mention key management explicitly — the synthesis catalogue
`99_CRA_Synthesis.md` §4 flags this as a T2-vs-text gap — so the
key-separation reading rests on the combination of Annex I (2)(d)
access-control duty with the Annex I (2)(e) "other technical means"
residual).

**Considerations.**
- The reading chosen anchors separation in functional role-based access
  control rather than physical separation: the key-material custodian and
  the operational-environment custodian are distinct roles with distinct
  credentials.
- The CRA state-of-the-art baseline + harmonised-standards anchor (Art. 27)
  governs the primitive choice; the CRA product-side key-separation
  baseline is the necessary-but-not-sufficient floor for DORA financial
  entities and the floor for CRA-only manufacturers.

**CSF anchors (from `SR-CRA-004` v0.3 security_rationale):**
PR.DS-01, PR.AA-05, GV.OV-01.
*(PR.DS-01 anchors the cryptographic protection so that possession of the
key — not the storage medium — is the necessary condition for
intelligibility; PR.AA-05 captures the access-control dimension with
distinct custodians for key-material and operational environments under
least-privilege entitlements; GV.OV-01 ensures the key-separation policy is
reviewed across the support period under the manufacturer's broader
risk-management strategy.)*

**Why Complementary**: GDPR's key-separation obligation is the mechanism that earns the Art. 34(3)(a) data-subject-notification carve-out: if the cryptographic key is unavailable to the adversary, the breached data is unintelligible, and notification is not required. CRA's key-separation obligation is a product-design invariant: the custodian holding the key material is not the same role as the operator consuming the data. The two converge on key separation as the technical property; they differ on the trigger (GDPR's breach-context carve-out vs CRA's design-time invariant). → full rationale: `../../../Regulation/GDPR/02_SecurityRules_NIST.md` SR-GDPR-003 + SR-GDPR-004; `../../../Regulation/CRA/02_SecurityRules_NIST.md` SR-CRA-004


<!-- /aegis:pair -->



##### Emergent tensions (3+ regulations)
<!-- emergent: GDPR,CRA,DORA -->

- All three regulations anchor key management to a **separation property** (custodian ≠ consumer), but the operational depth differs materially: GDPR (de-attribution test, PR.DS-01 + PR.IR-03); CRA (functional RBAC separation, PR.DS-01 + PR.AA-05 + GV.OV-01); DORA (HSM-anchored custodianship + split-knowledge + classification-driven cipher strength + quorum-based recovery, PR.DS-01 + PR.AA-03 + PR.AA-04 + GV.RM-04). A small SaaS company (CRA scope + GDPR scope) typically meets the floor; a DORA financial entity (all three) must operate a substantially deeper architecture. Proportionality (P2) is the relevant design lever.

<!-- /emergent -->

##### SR cross-validation
All three regulations share PR.DS-01 as a common CSF anchor; DORA additionally anchors on PR.AA-03 + PR.AA-04 (the strong-authentication + key-protection 3-control AND), and CRA additionally anchors on PR.AA-05 (least privilege) + GV.OV-01 (strategy review). GDPR additionally anchors on PR.IR-03 (resilience for the Art. 34(3)(a) carve-out). The cross-cutting observation: PR.DS-01 is the necessary but not sufficient anchor; the supplementary subcategories (PR.AA-*, PR.IR-*, GV.OV-*) encode the operational depth.

##### Downstream implication
For Phase 1C Doc 07, D-01.3 is the **deepest-stack** sub-domain within D-01: the DORA-classified key-management architecture is materially more expensive than the GDPR/CRA floor. Proportionality is decisive: a small SaaS (no DORA scope) meets the floor with HSM-anchored custodianship + documented rotation; a DORA financial entity requires the full split-knowledge + quorum-recovery stack.
