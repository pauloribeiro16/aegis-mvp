#### 4.4 CL5-1 — Art. 5(1) — Internal governance and control framework chapeau
**Source locus:** Art. 5(1).
> Financial entities shall have in place an **internal governance and control framework** that ensures an **effective and prudent** management of ICT risk, in accordance with Article 6(4), in order to achieve a high level of digital operational resilience.
**Instance 1 — VAG — S3 — `internal governance and control framework`**
The framework chapeau. Three materially distinct readings:
- **R1:** Three lines of defence model (per Art. 6(4) cross-reference).
- **R2:** Two lines of defence (business + control, common in some Member States).
- **R3:** Composite: any recognised internal-control model.
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Three lines of defence | In-Directive: Art. 6(4) `according to the three lines of defence model` |
| R2 | Two lines of defence | None in OJ |
| R3 | Any model | In-Directive: Art. 6(4) `or an internal risk management and control model` |
R3 is the literal reading (Art. 6(4) gives the option). S3 (the choice between R1 and R3 yields materially distinct control populations).
*Berry anchor:* §3.3.1 (`governance and control framework`); §5.4.7 (AND/OR structure).
**Instance 2 — VAG — S3 — `effective and prudent`**
Two vague qualifiers (AND). Both are Berry classics.
- **R1:** Effective (outcome-based) AND prudent (process-based, conservative).
- **R2:** Hendiadys (single concept: risk-management discipline).
- **R3:** Either, per entity judgment.

The target standard. Inherits DORA-D01 `digital operational resilience` (S3 `build, assure and review`).
- **R1:** All three lifecycle phases per R1 of DORA-D01.
- **R2:** Continuous process per R2 of DORA-D01.
- **R3:** All three control families per R3 of DORA-D01.

#### 4.5 CL5-2 — Art. 5(2) chapeau — Management body 4-verb coordination
**Source locus:** Art. 5(2), first subparagraph.
> The management body of the financial entity shall **define, approve, oversee and be responsible for** the implementation of all arrangements related to the ICT risk management framework referred to in Article 6(1).
**Instance 1 — COORD — S3 — `define, approve, oversee and be responsible for` (4-verb coordination)**
The **longest management-body coordination pattern in Ch. II**. Four verbs (AND) attached to the management body. Three materially distinct readings:
- **R1:** Four discrete actions (define a framework; approve it; oversee its execution; bear responsibility for outcomes).
- **R2:** Composite (a single management role covering all four aspects).
- **R3:** Two pairs: (define + approve = strategic), (oversee + responsible for = operational).
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Four discrete | In-Directive: literal four-verb coordination |
| R2 | Composite | None in OJ |
| R3 | Two pairs | None in OJ |
R1 is the literal reading. R1 vs R2 admit materially distinct compliance populations (R1: management body must produce four distinct artifacts/activities; R2: one composite activity suffices). S3.
*Berry anchor:* §5.4.7 (four-way coordination); §5.5 (verb parallelism); cf. pilot §2.1 (DORA-D01 `build, assure and review` — three verbs, same pattern).
**Instance 2 — VAG — S3 — `the management body`**
Inherits DORA-D30 `management body` (composite cross-reference stitching 5 sectoral definitions + national-law residual; see pilot file §2.10). The two readings:
- **R1:** Executive (running the entity day-to-day).
- **R2:** Supervisory (oversight, non-executive).
- **R3:** Both (per composite definition).

- **R3:** Formal arrangements only (the regulation's natural target).

#### 6.2 Art. 5(2) — the 4-verb coordination as DORA's governance signature
Art. 5(2) uses the **4-verb coordination `define, approve, oversee and be responsible for`** to describe the management body's role. This is the **longest management-body coordination pattern in DORA** and the operational analog of:
- **DORA-D01 `build, assure and review`** (3-verb, definition of digital operational resilience; pilot file §2.1)
- **Berry anchor:** §5.4.7 (4-way coordination); §5.5 (verb parallelism); §3.3.1 (`responsible for`).
- **Structural implication:** the 4-verb pattern is the **signature DORA governance clause**. It propagates to Art. 6(1) (framework chapeau — `sound, comprehensive and well-documented` is the framework's qualities, mirroring the management body's four duties), to Art. 5(2)(a) (`ultimate responsibility`), to Art. 5(2)(d) (`overall responsibility for setting and approving the digital operational resilience strategy`), and to Art. 6(10) (`fully responsible for the verification of compliance`).
#### 6.4 Art. 5(2)(a) and Art. 6(10) — non-delegable responsibility
Two clauses establish **non-delegable responsibility** of the management body / financial entity:
- **Art. 5(2)(a)** (CL5-3 Instance 1): `bear the ultimate responsibility for managing the financial entity's ICT risk`.
- **Art. 6(10)** (CL6-5 Instance 7): `the financial entity remains fully responsible for the verification of compliance with the ICT risk management requirements`.
The pattern is the **non-delegable accountability principle** — the management body cannot outsource the responsibility, even if it outsources the tasks. The principle is a **case-law concept** from banking supervision (Basel Committee) and EU financial-services law (CRD Art. 13, MiFID Art. 9 governance obligations).
- **Berry anchor:** §3.3.1 (`ultimate`, `fully`); §5.1 (`remains`).
- **Structural implication:** the non-delegable responsibility propagates to **out-sourcing** (Art. 28), **intra-group services** (Art. 28(4)), and **ICT third-party contracts** (Art. 30). A financial entity cannot escape Art. 5–6 obligations by contracting them out; the management body remains the accountable party.

### 3. Verbatim OJ text — Art. 24–27
#### 4.1 CL24-1 — Art. 24(1)+(2) chapeau — Programme establishment
**Source locus:** Art. 24(1) and 24(2).
> For the purpose of assessing preparedness for handling ICT-related incidents, of identifying weaknesses, deficiencies and gaps in digital operational resilience, and of promptly implementing corrective measures, financial entities, other than microenterprises, shall, taking into account the criteria set out in Article 4(2), **establish, maintain and review** a **sound and comprehensive digital operational resilience testing programme** as an integral part of the ICT risk-management framework referred to in Article 6.
**Instance 1 — VAG — S3 — `sound and comprehensive`**
The qualifier `sound and comprehensive` is undefined.
- **R1:** Strict reading: tests must cover every component and every modality (exhaustive).
- **R2:** Risk-proportional reading: tests must cover enough of the system to detect material weaknesses.
- **R3:** Methodology-based reading: tests must apply an industry-recognised methodology (e.g. NIST SP 800-115, OWASP WSTG, TIBER-EU).
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Exhaustive | None in OJ |
| R2 | Risk-proportional | In-Directive: Art. 4(2) proportionality |
| R3 | Methodology-based | None in OJ |

### from regs/DORA.ambiguity.md

The 4-verb coordination is **structurally distinct** from the 3-verb patterns in DORA-D01 and CRA-CL14 because it adds the `be responsible for` element — the **non-delegable accountability** anchor. ### from regs/DORA.ambiguity.md

- **Cross-regulation consistency:** the 4-verb pattern is **unique to DORA** in the 5-regulation corpus. NIS 2 Art. 20 (governance) uses single-verb obligations (`approve`, `oversee`). CRA Art. 13(1) uses 3 verbs. GDPR Art. 24 (controller responsibility) uses `demonstrate compliance` (single verb). AI Act Art. 14 (human oversight) uses 4 verbs but in a different configuration. DORA's 4-verb pattern is the most elaborate.


### from regs/DORA.ambiguity.md

NIS 2 has no equivalent (essential/important entities are not "governed" in the same way). CRA has no equivalent (manufacturers may delegate conformity assessment to notified bodies). GDPR Art. 24 (controller responsibility) has the same principle for data protection.
