#### 3.1 NIS2-CL01 — Art. 20(1) sentence 1 — Three-verb governance + liability
**Source locus:**
> Member States shall ensure that the management bodies of essential and important entities **<mark>approve the cybersecurity risk-management measures taken by those entities in order to comply with Article 21, oversee its implementation and can be held liable for infringements by the entities of that Article</mark>**.
**Instance 1 — VAG + POLY — S2 — `cybersecurity risk-management measures`**
The object of the three coordinated verbs is the same phrase as in Art. 21(1) opening. The Directive does not define `cybersecurity risk-management measures`; the term is composed of:
- `cybersecurity` (Art. 6(3), cross-referenced to Reg. 2019/881)
- `risk-management measures` (composite; no definition in Art. 6)
- `measures` (no definition; cf. Art. 21(1) `technical, operational and organisational measures`)
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | `measures` = the 10-item list of Art. 21(2)(a)–(j) | In-Directive: Art. 21(2) chapeau `shall include at least the following` (illustrative floor) |
| R2 | `measures` = whatever the entity adopts in fulfilment of Art. 21(1) opening | In-Directive: Art. 21(1) `appropriate and proportionate` suggests entity-discretionary |
| R3 | `measures` = implementing acts under Art. 21(5) (sectoral technical/methodological specs) | In-Directive: Art. 21(5) `implementing acts laying down the technical and the methodological requirements` |
R1 and R2 produce materially distinct obligations (R1 admits checklist-compliance; R2 requires entity-specific risk management). R3 is conditional on implementing acts adopted by 17 October 2024. S2.
*Berry anchor:* §3.3.1 (`measures`).
**Instance 2 — VAG — S2 — `in order to comply with Article 21`**
The purpose clause `in order to comply with Article 21` may be:
- **R1:** Purpose-indicative (explains why the measures are approved).
- **R2:** Purpose-restrictive (the approval obligation is bounded by Art. 21's scope).
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Indicative | None in OJ |
| R2 | Restrictive | None in OJ; recital 75 (management responsibility) |
R1 is the dominant reading. R2 is rejected in standard EU-drafting convention. Layer 1 catalogues the surface; the reading converges in practice.
*Berry anchor:* §5.1 (`in order to`).
**Instance 3 — COORD + POLY — S2 — three-verb coordination**
The sentence coordinates three verbs: `approve … oversee … can be held liable`.
Berry §5.5 parallelism test: the verbs are coordinated by AND (`approve, oversee and can be held liable`), with no explicit nested structure. Three readings:
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Three distinct obligations (each is independently required) | In-Directive: recital 75 implies distinct |
| R2 | One composite oversight duty (approve + oversee + liable together constitute the oversight) | None in OJ |
| R3 | Two distinct obligations (approve + oversee) and one consequence (held liable) | None in OJ |
R1 and R3 produce operationally distinct compliance paths (R1 admits piecemeal performance: "we approved but did not oversee"; R3 binds the acts of approval and oversight together). S2.
*Berry anchor:* §5.5 (three-way coordination).
**Instance 4 — VAG + POLY — S2 — `can be held liable for infringements`**
- `held liable` — civil, criminal, administrative, or all three? The Directive does not specify.
- `infringements` — which type? Infringements of Art. 21(1) (the opening) or Art. 21(2) (the list) or Art. 23 (reporting)?
| # | Reading (liability type) | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Civil liability (management body sued by entity or shareholders) | None in OJ; recital 75 |
| R2 | Criminal liability (prosecution) | None in OJ; recital 75 |
| R3 | Administrative liability (fines, disqualification) | None in OJ; recital 75 |
| R4 | All three (the Directive is silent; Member States decide) | None in OJ; recital 75 |
R4 is the dominant reading (the carve-out of Art. 20(1) sentence 2 explicitly defers to national law for public institutions, suggesting Member States otherwise have full latitude). The reading converges; the surface ambiguity is genuine.
| # | Reading (infringement scope) | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Infringements of Art. 21(1) opening | In-Directive: literal `of that Article` |
| R2 | Infringements of any Art. 21 sub-paragraph | In-Directive: purposive reading |
| R3 | Infringements of Art. 21 + Art. 23 (reporting) | None in OJ; recital 75 |
R1 is the literal reading; R2 is the dominant purposive reading; R3 imports reporting obligations by analogy. S2 because the difference is between which paragraphs trigger personal liability.
*Berry anchor:* §5.1 (`liable`, `infringements`).

#### 3.2 NIS2-CL02 — Art. 20(1) sentence 2 — Public-institution carve-out
**Source locus:**
> The application of this paragraph shall be **without prejudice to national law as regards the liability rules applicable to public institutions, as well as the liability of public servants and elected or appointed officials**.
**Instance 1 — SCOPE-Q — S2 — `public institutions`**
The carve-out applies to `public institutions`. The term is undefined in NIS 2. The reading:
- **R1:** `public institutions` = the entities designated as `public administration entities` in Art. 6(35).
- **R2:** `public institutions` = any entity owned or controlled by the State, broader than Art. 6(35).
- **R3:** `public institutions` = constitutional bodies (parliaments, central banks, judiciary).
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Art. 6(35) `public administration entity` | In-Directive: Art. 6(35) is the closest definition |
| R2 | Broader (any State-controlled entity) | None in OJ |
| R3 | Narrower (constitutional bodies only) | In-Directive: Art. 6(35) expressly excludes judiciary, parliaments, central banks |
R1 and R3 are the operative readings. R1 is broad (excludes constitutional bodies per Art. 6(35) chapeau); R3 is narrow (constitutional bodies are exempt from Art. 6(35) and thus from the Art. 20(1) carve-out's relevance). The reading affects whether state-owned enterprises (which are Art. 6(35)-excluded by the industrial/commercial-character test) are inside or outside the carve-out. S2.
*Berry anchor:* §5.1 (`public institutions` undefined).
**Instance 2 — VAG — S2 — `elected or appointed officials`**
The carve-out covers `elected or appointed officials`. The reading:
- **R1:** Officials elected or appointed to public office (politicians, ministers).
- **R2:** Officials appointed to positions of authority within public institutions (civil servants, agency heads).
- **R3:** Both.
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Elected officials only | None in OJ |
| R2 | Appointed officials only | None in OJ |
| R3 | Both | In-Directive: literal `elected or appointed` is inclusive OR |
R3 is the literal reading. S2 because the difference is between who is in the carve-out (R3 = all senior public officials; R1 = politicians only).
*Berry anchor:* §5.4.7 (OR).
**Layer 1 observation** — The Art. 20(1) carve-out defers to **national law** for the liability rules. This is the clearest single instance in NIS 2 of **Directive-vs-Regulation cross-border divergence potential**: a Dutch public-entity board member and a German public-entity board member face different liability exposures despite identical NIS 2 obligations. Flagged for Layer 2.

#### 3.6 NIS2-CL06 — Art. 20 chapeau — Implicit proportionality / scope hook
**Source locus (chapeau of Art. 20, paraphrased from sentence 1):**
> Member States shall ensure that [the obligations] apply to essential and important entities.
**Instance 1 — VAG + POLY — S2 — `essential and important entities`**
The term is defined in Art. 3 but Art. 3's own classification criteria (size thresholds, sectoral lists, Member State discretion) are open. Three layers of indeterminacy:
- Layer A: `essential` vs. `important` — different supervisory regimes (Art. 32 fines, Art. 33 enforcement measures).
- Layer B: size thresholds (medium vs. large per Recommendation 2003/361/EC).
- Layer C: sectoral coverage (Annex I/II).
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Two-tier binary (essential OR important) | In-Directive: Art. 3(1)–(2) |
| R2 | Member-State-discretionary boundary | In-Directive: Art. 3(1)(e), (g) — Member States may identify additional essential entities |
| R3 | Annex I/II sector lists are exhaustive | In-Directive: Art. 2(1) + Annex I/II |
R1 is the structural reading. R2 is the Member-State-discretion reading. R3 is the sectoral-exhaustive reading. S2 because the surface ambiguity drives whether an entity falls in the scope of Art. 20 (R1/R2/R3 may give different answers for borderline cases like cloud-computing providers with mixed sectoral exposure).
*Berry anchor:* §3.3.1 (`essential and important entities`); §5.2.1 (Annex lists).
**Layer 1 observation** — The Art. 3 classification of an entity is a **prerequisite** for all of Art. 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 obligations. If an entity is not classified as essential or important, none of these apply. The classification itself is ambiguous (R2: Member-State discretion; R3: Annex I/II exhaustive). This is the **single most important scope-defining layer of NIS 2** — flagged in synthesis.

#### 3.1 NIS2-CL07 — Art. 21(1) sentence 1 — Risk-management core obligation
**Source locus:**
> Member States shall ensure that essential and important entities take **<mark>appropriate and proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures</mark>** to manage the risks posed to the security of network and information systems which those entities use for their operations or for the provision of their services, and to prevent or minimise the **impact** of incidents on recipients of their services and on other services.
**Instance 1 — VAG — S3 — `appropriate and proportionate`**
The opening qualifier. Three materially distinct readings:
- **R1:** `appropriate` and `proportionate` are independent (entity must satisfy both tests, separately).
- **R2:** `appropriate` modifies `proportionate` (entity must satisfy proportionality, with `appropriate` as a placeholder synonym).
- **R3:** `appropriate and proportionate` is a single compound qualifier (a fixed phrase).
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Independent | None in OJ |
| R2 | Hierarchical | None in OJ |
| R3 | Compound | In-Directive: Art. 21(1) sentence 2 operationalises proportionality by specific factors (exposure, size, likelihood, severity) |
R1 and R3 produce materially distinct compliance obligations (R1 admits two separate failure modes; R3 admits one composite test). S3.
This is the dominant VAG trigger of NIS 2. **It propagates to all 10 sub-points of Art. 21(2)(a)–(j) and to Art. 21(3) and Art. 21(4).** Every measure listed in Art. 21(2) inherits the `appropriate and proportionate` qualifier by reference to "The measures referred to in paragraph 1".
*Berry anchor:* §5.1 (`appropriate`, `proportionate`).
**Instance 2 — POLY — S2 — `technical, operational and organisational measures`**
Three-term coordination. Three readings:
- **R1:** Three distinct categories of measures (entity must implement at least one of each).
- **R2:** Cumulative description (a measure is "technical, operational and organisational" if it has all three characteristics).
- **R3:** Open list (entity may add more categories).
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Distinct categories | In-Directive: literal `technical, operational and organisational` |
| R2 | Cumulative single measure | None in OJ |
| R3 | Open list | In-Directive: recital 56 (proportionality) |
R1 is the literal reading. R3 is the dominant reading (recital 56 implies entity-discretion on additional categories). S2.
*Berry anchor:* §5.5 (three-way coordination).
**Instance 3 — VAG + POLY — S2 — `manage the risks`**
`manage the risks` — what counts as `management`? Reading:
- **R1:** Identify + assess + mitigate.
- **R2:** Treat (avoid, accept, transfer, mitigate).
- **R3:** Continuous monitoring + adjustment.
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Identify-assess-mitigate | None in OJ; ISO/IEC 27005 |
| R2 | ISO 27005 risk-treatment | None in OJ; ISO/IEC 27005 |
| R3 | Continuous monitoring | None in OJ; NIST RMF |
*Berry anchor:* §3.3.1 (`manage`).
**Instance 4 — POLY — S2 — `impact`**
`impact` — on recipients of services (downstream effect) or on other services (cascading effect)?
- **R1:** Impact on direct recipients.
- **R2:** Impact on other services (cascade).
- **R3:** Both.
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Recipients | In-Directive: literal `recipients of their services` |
| R2 | Other services | In-Directive: literal `on other services` |
| R3 | Both | In-Directive: literal `recipients … and … other services` |
R3 is the literal reading (coordinated by `and`). The fact that the two objects are coordinated by AND rather than OR means the entity must consider impact on both. S2 because the surface ambiguity is between "focus on direct recipients" and "consider cascading effects".
*Berry anchor:* §3.3.1 (`impact`); §5.4.7 (AND).

### 4. Cross-clause structural observations (Art. 21)
3. **Three sub-points of Art. 21(2) coordinate three distinct security domains in a single phrase** ((c), (i), (j)). Berry §5.5 parallelism: the structural symmetry invites the reading that each domain is an independent obligation, but the clause does not signal whether partial compliance is acceptable. **This is the dominant S3 pattern in Art. 21.**
6. **`direct` in Art. 21(2)(d) is the single most consequential scope-limiter.** The reading determines whether the obligation reaches 1-hop, n-hop, or only contractual counter-parties. The Directive is silent; the Commission's 17 October 2024 implementing acts (Art. 21(5)) were expected to clarify this but as of mid-2025 are not yet published.
7. **T3-vs-text gaps are the most extensive in the corpus so far.** At least 6 sub-points (b, d, e, f, g, h, i, j) are split by T3 into multiple T3 clauses that the OJ text does not support. ### from regs/NIS2.ambiguity.md

1. **Art. 21(1) opening is the single most consequential VAG phrase.** `appropriate and proportionate` propagates to every sub-point of Art. 21(2)(a)–(j), to Art. 21(3), and to Art. 21(4). Every clause in Art. 21 inherits S3 VAG on this opening qualifier. **This is structurally identical to GDPR Art. 32(1) opening's propagation to Art. 32(1)(a)–(d), and to CRA Annex I Part I §2 opening.**

### from regs/NIS2.ambiguity.md

2. **Art. 21(2) is structured as a 10-item list, but each item is a single phrase or short clause.** The operational content is delegated almost entirely to standards and national implementation. This makes Layer 1 findings uniformly S2-S3 — the text is too thin to generate the dense coordination ambiguities seen in GDPR Art. 4 (which has 26 definitions) or in some CRA Annex I items (which have longer compound requirements).

### from regs/NIS2.ambiguity.md

4. **`state-of-the-art` propagation is the cross-regulation Berry-classic.** Same term in GDPR Art. 25(1) and Art. 32(1) and NIS 2 Art. 21(1) sentence 2. The three regulations have begun to diverge in national transposition: German BSI standardises the term, French ANSSI requires independent assessment, Italian ACN publishes annual guidance. The Directive's silence on the term is the **single most actionable gap for harmonisation**.

### from regs/NIS2.ambiguity.md

5. **`where appropriate` is the soft-hedge pattern of Art. 21.** Three sub-points are closed by `where appropriate` (h, j — and the chapeau of (j)). The hedge softens the obligation without specifying the threshold. **Cross-regulation comparison:** GDPR Art. 32(1) `where appropriate` and CRA Art. 13(5) `as appropriate` follow the same pattern.

### from regs/NIS2.ambiguity.md

8. **The "physical environment" scope extension is the most distinctive feature of NIS 2 vs other regulations in the corpus.** All other regulations (GDPR, CRA, DORA, AI Act) are software/cyber-focused. NIS 2's all-hazards + physical-environment coverage is the unique outlier. **For software-compliance analysis, the physical-environment dimension is out-of-scope** — it does not generate software requirements.
