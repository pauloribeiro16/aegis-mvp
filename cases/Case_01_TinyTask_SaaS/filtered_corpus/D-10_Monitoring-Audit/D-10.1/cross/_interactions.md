## Cross-recipient map

#### Cross-recipient map
D-10.1 generates a layered recipient set on the **continuous-monitoring evidence trail**. The recipients are at distinct regulatory channels on the same monitoring-data object but the channels do not generate a notification cascade of D-10.1 itself — they are **monitoring-evidence channels** that feed distinct regulatory artefacts:
- **GDPR** — data subject + supervisory authority (DPA) on the Art. 32(2) monitoring evidence + Art. 33(1) breach-`becoming-aware` clock.
- **NIS 2** — management body + competent authority (CSIRT or NCA) on the Art. 21(2)(b) incident-handling detection capability (partial-coverage dimension).
- **CRA** — market surveillance authority (MSA) + users on the Annex I Part I (2)(l) on-device monitoring with user opt-out + Annex I Part II (6) vulnerability-reporting contact address.
- **DORA** — national competent authority (NCA) on the Art. 8(1) continuous monitoring (OJ-literal locus Art. 9(1)) + Art. 10(1)+(2) prompt anomalous-activity detection.
- **AI Act** — market surveillance authority (MSA) + AI Office + provider on the Art. 72(1)–(2) post-market monitoring system + Art. 74 market-surveillance cooperation.
These are distinct regulatory channels on the monitoring-data object. **The cross-recipient map does NOT generate a notification cascade of D-10.1 itself** — the continuous-monitoring duty is upstream of any breach notification (cross to D-04.3 Regulatory Notification, D-04.4 Data Restoration & Recovery). The D-10.1 monitoring evidence feeds the D-04 incident-response chain (D-04.1 Detection & Triage, D-04.2 Containment & Mitigation, D-04.3 Regulatory Notification, D-04.4 Data Restoration & Recovery) and the D-09.4 Records of Processing documentation obligation. **The CRDA-flagged CRA-opt-out vs. mandatory-monitoring tension has no recipient — the conflict is between the regimes' substance, not their recipients.**

## Scope-overlap principle (operational)

#### Scope-overlap principle (operational)
The high-level SO (`SO-D-10.1.HL`) engages on a given party **if and only if ≥2 applicable regulations have ≥1 Y or Conditional-Y-scope-overlap pair on the same party** (e.g. a DORA financial entity that is also a controller processing personal data through high-risk AI — a vertically-integrated financial-controller-AI-provider with GDPR + DORA + AI Act applicability on the layered 5-monitoring-architecture). For a CRA-regulated manufacturer that is also a controller (controller-manufacturer), the GDPR + CRA sub-SOs activate on **two different artefacts** (entity-level 5-risk-type monitoring vs. product-level on-device internal-activity monitoring) — the two regimes meet at the controller-manufacturer interface, **but the CRDA-flagged GENUINE TENSION (CRA user opt-out vs. GDPR mandatory monitoring) requires Layer 2 OJ-level resolution**. For an entity with GDPR + CRA + DORA applicability, the three sub-SOs activate with the CRA opt-out conflict propagating against both GDPR and DORA mandatory monitoring. The five monitoring mandates are stacked, not collapsed: 5-risk-type detection (GDPR) + all-hazards incident-handling detection capability (NIS 2 partial) + on-device internal-activity monitoring with user opt-out (CRA) + continuous ICT-system monitoring (DORA) + AI-system post-market monitoring (AI Act). The high-level SO surfaces the common continuous-monitoring duty but does **NOT** collapse the per-reg anchor, scope dimension, trigger, threshold, recipient, or content template, **and does NOT resolve the CRDA-flagged GENUINE TENSION**.

## Emergent tensions (from CRDA-deep §D-10.1)

#### Emergent tensions (from CRDA-deep §D-10.1)
**1 conditional emergent tension** (CRDA-flagged GENUINE TENSION — Layer 2 OJ-level resolution required):

For a CRA-regulated product processing personal data, the user's opt-out from CRA product-level monitoring does not legally exempt the controller from GDPR Art. 32(2) entity-level mandatory detection.
